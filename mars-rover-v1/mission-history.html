Overview

This timeline highlights major mission milestones and achievements from NASA's Mars rover program. Each rover contributed scientific discoveries and engineering lessons that advanced our understanding of Mars and informed later missions.

Mission Timeline

Sojourner (Pathfinder)

1997

Sojourner was the first successful Mars rover (part of the Mars Pathfinder mission). It demonstrated the feasibility of remote robotic surface exploration and returned the first close-up images of Martian rocks and soil.

Spirit & Opportunity (Mars Exploration Rovers)

2004–2010+

Spirit and Opportunity landed in 2004 and vastly exceeded their planned 90-sol missions. They returned evidence of past water activity and diverse rock chemistries, reshaping ideas about Mars' wetter past.

Curiosity (Mars Science Laboratory)

2012–present

Curiosity landed in Gale Crater in 2012 with a large suite of instruments to study past habitability. It analyzed rock chemistry and environmental conditions and continues to provide a long-running climate and geology record.

Perseverance & Ingenuity

2021–present

Perseverance landed in Jezero Crater in 2021 to collect samples for future return to Earth and search for biosignatures. Ingenuity, the small helicopter, accomplished the first powered flights on another planet.

Future Missions (Sample Return & Beyond)

Plans for sample return and follow-up missions build on Perseverance's cached samples and pave the way for future robotic and human exploration of Mars.

Selected Milestones

  • 1997: Sojourner demonstrates rover mobility on Mars.
  • 2004: Spirit and Opportunity land and begin extended missions.
  • 2012: Curiosity lands and begins detailed geochemical investigations.
  • 2021: Perseverance lands, collects samples, and Ingenuity completes first flights.
